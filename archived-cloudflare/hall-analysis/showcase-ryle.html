Corpus Note

John Charles Ryle was the first Bishop of Liverpool (1880–1900), prolific Evangelical Anglican writer and preacher. His key works — Holiness, Expository Thoughts on the Gospels, Old Paths — represent the Victorian Evangelical tradition at its plainest and most direct. Deliberately anti-ornamental: "I write plainly because I want to be useful to plain people." His preaching is doctrine stripped of rhetorical embellishment.

The Ryle Signature

Ryle's signature is direct, unadorned doctrinal address. He never uses a complex sentence when a simple one will do, never uses an illustration when the doctrine itself is vivid enough. His is the anti-Spurgeon school: where Spurgeon soars, Ryle marches — steadily, directly, with a soldier's economy. But the content is as theologically rich as any in the Hall. He proves that plainness can be a spiritual discipline.
